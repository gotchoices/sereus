# An invitation cannot be cancelled, so member removal is not a re-entry gate

Found during review of `bug-strand-member-delete-unauthorized`, which added signature-gated
member removal. That gate works. What it does not do — and cannot, on its own — is stop a
removed party from walking straight back in.

## What was measured

Reproduced against a real closed strand and pinned as a passing test in
`packages/cadre-core/test/strand-member-revocation.spec.ts`
(`'a revoked member re-admits ITSELF off an invite it held but never consumed'`):

- A manager issues two invitations to the same person.
- That person joins using the first one, and is later removed by the manager.
- That person consumes the second, still-unused invitation and is a member again — no
  manager action, no further approval, nothing the manager can do to stop it.

The removal gate's own anti-re-entry rule only covers the invitation that was *already
spent*: a spent invitation's consumption record is stale and no longer re-admits anyone.
An *unspent* invitation is untouched by removal.

## Why it happens

An invitation is a bearer credential. Whoever holds its private key can redeem it. The
`Strand.Invite` table has:

- no way to mark an invitation cancelled, spent-for-good, or otherwise dead — the table
  is insert-only and carries a standing `TODO: ability to deactivate?`,
- no binding to a specific invitee,
- only an optional expiry, which the issuer may set far in the future or leave null (never
  expires).

## Why this matters more than "invitations are bearer tokens"

The sharp case is a **manager anticipating its own removal**. Managers are the parties that
issue invitations. A manager can mint a stack of never-expiring invitations for itself,
then be demoted and removed — and re-seat itself as a member at will, indefinitely, with no
remaining party able to revoke that ability. The strand has no mechanism to take it back.

## Expected behavior

Removing a member should be able to actually keep that member out. Concretely, an operator
needs at least:

- a way to **cancel an outstanding invitation** — so a manager can neutralize invitations a
  removed party may be holding,
- ideally a way to see **which invitations are outstanding**, since an operator cannot cancel
  what it cannot enumerate,
- and a decision on whether removing a member should cancel invitations **bound to that
  member** — which in turn raises whether invitations should be bound to an intended invitee
  at issue time at all, rather than being pure bearer credentials.

Whether cancellation is a new deactivation record, a state column, or a rethink of invitations
as invitee-bound rather than bearer, is open. Note that the existing `NotExpired` check reads
`context.Now`, supplied by the writer — any new "is this invitation still live?" rule should
avoid widening the surface where a caller-supplied value decides admission.
