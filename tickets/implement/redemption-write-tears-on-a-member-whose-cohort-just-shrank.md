# A device's admission write must be one the device itself can hold

## What was found

Reproduced 4 of 4 times on 2026-10-07 (the implement pass's one observation plus three full runs of a scratch copy of `cadre-invite-any-member` with one member M instead of two). Traces: `tickets/.logs/redemption-write-tears.lone.run2.log` (full trace, owner side included) and `...run4.log` (adds the owner's raw block index and a second owner restart). Both are pruned with the rest of that directory; the facts below are what they showed.

**The cohort grows by the device, it does not shrink.** The scenario waits until the stopped owner A has left M's control cohort (about 5 s), so M is alone when P starts. The moment P connects to M to redeem, M's key network classifies P as serving the control network (every control node registers the cluster and repo protocols, and on a ring narrower than the replication breadth of 16 every serving node is in every cohort), and M's cohort for every control block becomes {M, P}. P holds no block of any control collection.

**The write tears because P cannot hold it.** `redeemCadreInvite` is one transaction over `CadrePeer`, `OwnerKey` and `CadreInviteUsage`. Optimystic pends each collection on {M, P}, and M's coordinator acknowledges a commit only when more than half the cohort reports holding it. For `CadrePeer` (revision 6) and `OwnerKey` (revision 2), P's storage refuses the commit with `missing-base-revision` ("local latest none"): it has no earlier revision to apply the transform to, and a member never fetches a base under its write latch by design. M holds the write, so `durableHolders` is 1 of 2 and the coordinator answers `commit-not-durable`. `CadreInviteUsage` commits on both: the collection is fresh on both nodes (each staged an empty one), so P has no base to miss. The coordinator re-drives the refused collections for about 20 s, but P never heals in that window: M's per-stream gate denies P's repo, sync and cluster streams (`authorizeInboundControlStream: DENYING … not in the materialized authorized set`), and M's peer-join block catch-up pushes only to authorized members. The last round settles the write as `TornActionError … completion-refused, final=false` and the whole thing surfaces as `CoordinatorPartialCommitError` with the usage collections committed and the membership collections failed. The handler answers `internal` (retryable), as the classifier intends for a possibly-stored write.

**The retry succeeds without a second usage row.** M's own storage committed every block of the torn write; only the acknowledgement was refused. When P retries 2 s later with a fresh stamp, `readSeatedAdmission` finds P's `CadrePeer` and `OwnerKey` rows and answers `alreadyMember: true` before the seat check runs. One usage row exists afterwards, the use cap holds, and `verifyInvitationAdmission` accepts the admitted rows on M. The fix ticket's worry about a spent seat and a second usage row did not materialize.

**The returning owner never lists P because the member is on a lineage nobody certified.** M's revision 6 of `CadrePeer` was committed locally without a durable majority and so carries no cohort commit proof. Once M's membership snapshot includes P, M pushes that revision to P, so P and M share it. The owner A restarts holding revision 5. In run 4, A's startup self-record update committed at revision 6 on A's own lineage before M's push reached it, and the collection then reports `lineage-divergence forkRev=6` between M's action and A's; a second restart of A over the same storage changed nothing, and A's raw store showed its own revisions 6 to 8. In run 2, M rejected A's self-record update with `content-digest-mismatch` instead, and although M's push then landed revision 6 in A's storage, A's membership read did not show P for 90 s. Either way the owner is stuck on a different history of `CadrePeer` from the member. This is the class `tickets/blocked/forked-control-collection-sync-livelocks.md` owns, appended there as "Third trigger"; it is not worked here, because the fix below keeps the fork from forming.

**Why two members pass.** With M and N the cohort is {M, N, P}: two of three hold the write, P's refusal is tolerated, the commit is acknowledged with a proof, and P catches up after it is admitted.

## The fix: catch the device up before admitting it

On the member, in `CadreInviteHandler.seatAndRedeem`, after the request's checks have passed (party, connecting identity equals `peerKey`, both signatures, target device) and the invitation row is seated, and before `redeemCadreInvite`: push this member's control blocks to the device and wait for the push to finish, then write. The device then holds the base revision of every control block, so when it sits in the member's write cohort the commit lands on both and is acknowledged with a proof on the first attempt.

A prototype of exactly this (a `catchUpDevice` callback on the handler options; `CadreNode` adds the device's peer id to a set the control backfill's `authorizePeer` consults, calls `PeerJoinBackfill.catchUpPeer` for it, and removes it afterwards) was measured 3 of 3 in the one-member shape: the push offered and landed 26 blocks in 62 to 68 ms over loopback, P was admitted on attempt 1 about 410 ms after it first asked, the owner listed P within 5.1 s of reconnecting, and the traces held zero `commit-not-durable`, torn or partial-commit lines (`tickets/.logs/redemption-write-tears.lone.proto{1,2,3}.log`). The prototype was reverted; this ticket is the landing.

**Pieces.**

- `CadreInviteHandlerOptions.catchUpDevice?: (peerId: string) => Promise<void>`, called between the seat and the redeem. A failed or throwing catch-up is logged and the redemption proceeds: the write then either commits (the device healed another way) or tears and is retried as today.
- `CadreNode` keeps a set of peer ids whose verified redemption is in progress. `startControlBackfill`'s `authorizePeer` admits a peer in that set as it admits an authorized member. The catch-up method adds the id, awaits `controlBackfill.catchUpPeer(peerIdFromString(id))`, and removes the id in a `finally`. Wire it into the handler's construction in `start`.
- `catchUpPeer` returns an empty result when a run for that peer is already in flight, and a run the device's `peer:identify` scheduled a moment earlier (debounced, then denied because the device was not authorized) can be that run. Make the catch-up wait for an in-flight run and run again when that run was denied, or have `catchUpPeer` do so; otherwise the device is admitted with no blocks and the write tears exactly as before, rarely. A denied run sets no backoff and is not memoized as done, so a fresh run is allowed.
- The push goes to a device that holds a live, owner-signed invitation and has proven possession and consent in-protocol. If the write then fails (the last seat taken by a race, the row expiring between the check and the write), the device keeps a copy of the control store it was not admitted to. That is the same information a seed hands an un-enrolled machine, and the owner chose to invite this holder; record it as an accepted-tradeoff `NOTE:` at the call site, with the revisit condition "if a control table ever carries data a non-member must not see". Checking the seat count before the push (`countCadreInviteUsage` against `totalUses`) narrows the window cheaply when the store surface allows it.
- The push direction is member to device over the connection the device opened, so neither node's inbound stream gate changes: the member's gate is not consulted for its own outbound push, and the device's authorized set is empty at that point, which admits the member's block-transfer stream.

**Why not the fix ticket's proposal (answer `busy` while the cohort is small or just shrank).** The cohort did not shrink during the write; it grew by the device, and a member alone with a device will always have the device in its cohort. Refusing on that would refuse the one-member household shape forever.

**Why not upstream.** Cohort assembly is optimystic's, and its backlog `feat-declared-membership-feeds-cohort-assembly` would let the control network hand it an authenticated member list, which would keep an unadmitted device out of the cohort. That is far off and would also keep the device out after admission until the list refreshes. The member's `missing-base-revision` refusal is by design (no fetch under the write latch; healing is out of band), and the thing that blocks the healing here is this repository's stream gate. Handing the device the blocks it needs is the answer on this side.

## Scenario

Switch `cadre-invite-any-member.integration.ts` to one member M (`members.push(await startMember(aOwnerKey))` once): the owner-offline redemption at the only always-on member is the shape the feature exists for, and the two-member shape was chosen only to step around this defect. Drop the "other member sees the rows by replication" arm with it. Keep `redeemWithRetry`, and pin that P is admitted on attempt 1: the catch-up makes the first attempt deterministic (3 of 3), and a retry would mean the catch-up raced its in-flight run. Rewrite the header's "Why this shape" paragraph: the device's arrival in the member's cohort and the catch-up that answers it replace the paragraph about the torn write. The sibling implement ticket `phone-owner-never-leaves-members-cohort` later makes A phone-shaped and says to keep N until this ticket lands; with this landing, that instruction is satisfied.

## Docs

In `docs/architecture.md` → "Enrollment Flow: Invitation Redeemed at Any Member": state the constraint as a fact of the design, not a history. A device that connects to a member to redeem joins that member's control write cohort at once; a cohort of two commits only when both hold the write; so the member pushes its control store to the device after the request verifies and before it writes, and the admission then lands on the first attempt with the owner offline. Add the step to the sequence diagram (between 7 and the write). In the gate's "What it admits anyway" paragraph, note that the one push to a not-yet-member is this one, gated on a verified redemption. Mention the accepted tradeoff in one line and stop.
