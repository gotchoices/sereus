# The strand revocation enforcer wakes on a membership change

## Why

The revoked-peer enforcer (`strand-revocation-enforcer.ts`) keeps, per closed strand, the set of peer ids whose `Strand.MemberPeer` binding has no live `Strand.Member` row, refuses them at the libp2p gates, and hangs up their open connections. Today it rebuilds that set by scanning `MemberPeer` and `Member` every 30 s.

Measured in the GitHub #50 comment (two relay-only parties through relay.sereus.org, 120 s idle): about 33 relayed `db-p2p/sync/1.0.0` requests per strand per two minutes, 83–98% of them within 2 s of a `deny set refreshed` log line. The `message-cost-accounting` baseline (idle 60 s, strand case) attributes 18 sync requests to the enforcer. On a Galaxy S7 it is the phone's main idle traffic, about 8 inbound sync requests a minute per strand, and it grows linearly with strands held.

The cause: each scan reads the collection's header and log tail. A held block read more than 10 s after its last check starts a background `/sync` to every other cohort member (Optimystic lazy read repair, `readRepairWindowMs` 10 s). Commits this node received as a cohort member do not refresh that window, so every 30 s refresh finds all four blocks stale.

## Design

### Schedule

The enforcer runs every refresh on one `TableChangeWatch` (`table-change-watch.ts`), the way `PendingJoinRunner` does (`pending-join-runner.ts` → `subscribe`, `retryPass`):

- **On a change** to `Strand.Member` or `Strand.MemberPeer`, through `StrandDatabase.watchTables`. A burst of commits (a join writes `Member`, then `MemberPeer`; the reconciler's own writes) collapses into one refresh through the seam's 50 ms coalescing and single-flight rule.
- **Once at start**, as `trigger(0)`.
- **A backstop** `DEFAULT_REVOCATION_BACKSTOP_MS` = 5 min after the last refresh settled (the watch's `backstopMs`).
- **After a failed refresh**, `trigger(REVOCATION_REFRESH_RETRY_MS)` = 30 s. Today's 30 s poll is the retry for a failed read; keep that bound. This matters most for the first refresh: the Optimystic plugin arms a table's replicated-change bridge only on that table's first full read on this `Database` (the NOTE on `watchTables`), so until a refresh succeeds, replicated removals would raise nothing.
- **Wide strands:** after a successful refresh whose snapshot has more distinct bound peer ids than the strand's cluster size, `trigger(REVOCATION_WIDE_STRAND_REFRESH_MS)` = 30 s. On such a strand this node may be outside a membership block's cohort, so it takes in no storage event for a removal (docs/architecture.md → "Table change subscriptions", what the seam does not hear). This keeps today's 30 s bound there instead of loosening it to 5 min. The count is from the enforcer's own snapshot (orphaned bindings included, which only errs toward refreshing more often). The cluster size comes in as a new dep (`cohortBreadth`), from the `strandClusterSize` that `buildStrandRuntime` already resolves.
- **`refresh()`** stays public, serialized through `refreshTail` as now, and still resolves only after the sweep. The watch's `onChange` calls it.

Remove the `refreshing` flag and the skip-a-tick logic: the watch's single-flight rule replaces them. Replace `RevocationRefreshScheduler` / `defaultScheduler` with an optional `scheduler?: TimeoutScheduler` dep passed to the watch, plus an optional `watchChanges?(onChange, options): TableChangeWatch` dep. When `watchChanges` is absent, as in unit specs, fall back to `scheduleRuns`. Drop `RevocationRefreshScheduler` from `index.ts`.

### Config

`StrandRevocationEnforcementConfig` becomes:

- `enabled?: boolean`, unchanged.
- `backstopMs?: number`, replacing `pollIntervalMs` (renamed: it is no longer a poll). Default `DEFAULT_REVOCATION_BACKSTOP_MS`, which replaces `DEFAULT_REVOCATION_POLL_INTERVAL_MS` with no alias.
- `wakeOnChange?: boolean`, default true. False means no table subscription (use `scheduleRuns`): refreshes happen only at start, on the backstop, on retry and on `refreshRevocationEnforcement`. It exists for integration scenarios that need a machine's cuts to be the ones the test drove (see Integration scenarios). Document that a production node should leave it on.

### Starting at publication

The enforcer reads `instance.database`, which `StrandInstanceManager.publishDatabase` sets. On a joiner held by the first-sync gate, that happens after `buildStrandRuntime` has already called `revocationEnforcer?.start()`, so today's first refresh fails with "no live database" and waits 30 s. A table subscription also needs the published database. So:

- Remove `revocationEnforcer?.start()` (~988) from `buildStrandRuntime`.
- In `publishDatabase`, next to the reconciler kick, call `this.revocationEnforcers.get(strandId)?.start()`. Non-gated launches publish inside `buildStrandDatabase`. Gated ones publish from the gate's `onHeaderHeld` or `ensureFounderBootstrap`. A deferred join (`'awaiting-seed'`) publishes on its database retry. A hibernation wake re-runs `buildStrandRuntime` with the retained launch config and publishes again. All of these go through this one call.
- The `watchChanges` dep closure reads `instance.database` (set immediately before `start()`) and calls `watchTables(['Strand.Member', 'Strand.MemberPeer'], …)`. If the subscription throws, `start()` logs it and falls back to `scheduleRuns`, keeping the backstop, retry and wide-strand refreshes. `publishDatabase` must stay synchronous and must not throw (it resolves writable waiters).
- `stop()` disposes the watch. `StrandDatabase.close()` also disposes it through its `TableWatchRegistry`. Dispose is idempotent.

Before publication the deny set is empty either way (there was no database to read), so starting later loses nothing.

### The membership reconciler gets its own default

Add `DEFAULT_MEMBERSHIP_RECONCILE_INTERVAL_MS = 30_000` in `strand-membership-reconciler.ts` as its own default (idle cadence and ladder cap, unchanged in value), and stop importing the enforcer's constant. The reconciler stops by itself once joined, so its cadence matters only while joining. Update its module doc and config doc, the `StartStrandConfig.membershipReconciliation` doc, and `types.ts` (~915).

### Docs and comments that state the old behaviour

Several comments say a local `revokeMember` / `leaveStrand` "raises no event the runtime can hook". That is no longer true. Apps write through `instance.database.getDatabase()`, the same Quereus `Database` the watch is on, so a local removal wakes the enforcer within the coalescing window. A replicated removal wakes it when this node's storage takes it in. Rewrite:

- the enforcer module doc ("Removal cuts LIVE sessions too": the refresh is still the teardown seam, but now runs on change), the `start` doc, and the `readStrandRevocationRows` NOTE ("every 30 s" becomes "on each membership change and the backstop");
- `revokeMember` / `leaveStrand` docs, `CadreNode.refreshRevocationEnforcement` and `StrandInstanceManager.refreshRevocationEnforcement` docs. The call is now for a caller that wants to await the cut (it resolves after the sweep), not for promptness;
- `types.ts` ~900.

Put a `NOTE:` at `DEFAULT_REVOCATION_BACKSTOP_MS` naming the two paths the backstop covers: a machine that was offline when the removal was committed (it normally gets the blocks by join backfill or read repair on reconnect, which also raise the storage event), and anything the seam misses. Say the wide-strand refresh covers the outside-the-cohort case. Put a `NOTE:` at the `watchChanges` subscription naming the read-repair self-wake and why it stops after one refresh: a refresh's read can start a read-repair sync whose landing raises a change. The follow-up refresh runs within the coalescing window plus one read, well inside the 10 s read-repair window, so it starts no second sync. If idle sync counts do not fall, suspect this first.

## Settled choices

- **Do not widen `readRepairWindowMs` instead.** That would delay, for every read on the strand, how quickly a reader notices a commit it missed, only to save a poller that should not be polling.
- **Rename `pollIntervalMs` to `backstopMs`.** The project carries no backwards compatibility yet, and a field named for polling that no longer polls misleads. Only the harness and the two scenarios set it.
- **`wakeOnChange` is a real config field, not a test hook.** The scenarios need a machine whose deny set moves only when the test drives it. The enforcer is built inside `StrandInstanceManager` from config, so there is no other injection point. Its cost is one boolean.
- **The wide-strand 30 s refresh is in scope.** Without it, a removal on a strand wider than the cluster size would take up to 5 min to bite on machines outside the membership blocks' cohort, where today it takes 30 s. That would loosen a security-relevant bound without anyone noticing. The two-party case measured in #50 (two machines, cluster size 4) never takes this path.

## Edge cases & interactions

- **Gated joiner.** The enforcer starts at `publishDatabase`, not before. Verify by inspection that every publish path (non-gated build, `onHeaderHeld`, `ensureFounderBootstrap`, deferred-join retry) reaches the new `start()`. `start()`'s `started` / `stopped` guards make a second call, or a call after `releaseRuntime` stopped it, a no-op.
- **Stop during build.** `buildStrandDatabase` publishes synchronously after its gate-identity check. A runtime released mid-build has already deleted its enforcer from the map, so `get(strandId)` finds nothing or finds a stopped one. Inspection.
- **Hibernation.** Quiesce runs `releaseRuntime`: the enforcer is stopped, and `StrandDatabase.close()` disposes the watch. A wake rebuilds both through `buildStrandRuntime` → `publishDatabase`. The existing hibernation and instance-manager specs cover the wake. Read `hibernation-manager.ts`'s wake path to confirm it goes through `resumeStrand`.
- **Open strands** build no enforcer and so take no subscription. Unchanged.
- **First refresh fails.** The retry trigger re-reads in 30 s, and that read is also what arms the plugin's replicated-change bridge. Covered by the rewritten enforcer spec below.
- **Explicit `refresh()` during a watch run.** Both chain on `refreshTail`, so no two reads overlap. The watch keeps only its own runs to one at a time. Inspection.
- **Self-wake.** The enforcer writes nothing, and `hangUp` writes nothing. A read-repair landing caused by its own read gives at most one extra refresh (see the NOTE above). Inspection, and the measurement.
- **Reconciler writes wake the enforcer.** A join's `Member` and `MemberPeer` writes wake one or two refreshes. Intended: that is the moment a re-admitted party leaves the deny set.
- **Wide strand.** More distinct bound peer ids than `cohortBreadth` gives a 30 s follow-up after each successful refresh. At or below it, none. Covered by the rewritten spec.
- **Subscription throws.** `start()` falls back to `scheduleRuns` and logs. It never throws out of `publishDatabase`. Inspection.
- **`wakeOnChange: false`.** No subscription. Refreshes run at start, on the backstop, on retry, on the wide-strand trigger and on demand. The integration scenarios exercise it.
- **Many strands.** Each strand's watch is independent. Idle cost per strand becomes the backstop's four syncs per 5 min. The measurement with 1 and 3 strands shows it.
