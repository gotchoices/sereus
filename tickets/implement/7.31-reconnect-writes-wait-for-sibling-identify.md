# A reconnecting machine waits for its sibling to identify before writing as connected

Replaces `fix/membership-view-keeps-rows-a-deferred-consult-left-stale`. That ticket blamed Optimystic deferring consults past its cap of 16; on today's tree the failure it measured has a different cause, below.

## What happens

`CadreNode` treats "a control connection is open" as "my writes reach the cohort". Optimystic does not. Its cohort for a block (`Libp2pKeyPeerNetwork.findCluster` → `assembleServingCohort`) admits only peers whose peer-store record lists the control network's protocols, which libp2p fills in when its identify exchange completes. Cadre's own `isNetworkMember` hook (`isControlNetworkMember || admittingDevices.has`) narrows it further. Between `connection:open` and that peer's `peer:identify`, which took tens of milliseconds to about a second in these runs, the cohort is this node alone.

Three things fire on `connection:open` today:

- `handleControlConnectionChange`: the 0→≥1 growth edge, which starts the write-while-alone drain. On a node's first growth after start, the drain always re-touches its own `CadrePeer` row (`runDrainControlReplication` step 2).
- `onControlSiblingConnectionChange('open')`: drops the membership views, arms the second drop 11 s later, and triggers a reconcile pass. That pass runs `publishFirstSelfRecord`, whose only connected check is `getControlConnectionCount() > 0`.
- `committedAlone()`, the write-while-alone test, is `getConnections().length === 0`, so it also counts a relay, a bootstrap node, or a peer that has not identified yet as company.

A restarted machine's re-touch therefore commits on a cohort of one (`commit:solo-cohort`, `cohortSize: 1`, `quorum: 'local'`, `knownAbsent: 0`). `committedAlone()` says it was not alone, so nothing is queued. If the sibling advanced the same block to the same revision number while this machine was away, the two copies are a fork: same revision, different rows. Neither side's later consult sees it, because the consult compares revision numbers only (`cluster-fetch:local-current`, `localRev: 6, clusterRev: 6`, `claimants: 1` on both A and M). The peer-join catch-up push does not see it either, because the receiver keeps the higher revision and the two are equal. The fork is permanent until some later write by this machine to that block conflicts with the sibling's revision and its retry pulls the sibling's copy.

## Measured

Scenario `cadre-invite-any-member`: owner A stops; device P joins at member M, which writes P's `CadrePeer`, `CadreInvite` and `CadreInviteUsage` rows; A restarts and must list P within 60 s. To reproduce on today's tree, apply the three edits `implement/invited-machine-skips-its-first-publish` describes (boot timer and `publishFirstSelfRecord` call `publishSelfRecordUnlessCurrent()`, and `redeemCadreInvitation` calls `rememberSignedSelfRecord(record, false)` before `redeemInPasses`). The view's maximum age is 15 s now, not the 2 minutes the old ticket assumed.

- With those edits: 3 of 4 runs failed at 60 s, and 1 passed at 40.9 s (2026-10-20).
- At the timeout, A's raw storage (`ControlDatabase.countRows`, which reads storage rather than the view) held `CadrePeer` 2 rows against M's 3, `CadreInviteUsage` 0 against 1, and `CadreInvite` 1 against 2. `OwnerKey`, `OwnerGrant` and `OwnerGrantUsage` matched. So it is A's storage that lacks the rows, not only its view.
- Debug trace (`DEBUG='sereus:cadre:node,optimystic:db-p2p:coordinator-repo*'`): A's first-growth drain started on `connection:open` to M. Its `registerSelf` refresh committed `commit:solo-cohort` on A's `CadrePeer` data block (rev 5→6) 23 ms later. M held rev 6 with P's row. Every later consult of that block, on both machines, ended `local-current` at rev 6. A never read `CadreInvite` or `CadreInviteUsage` after restarting, because the invitation chain loads only when some `CadrePeer` row was admitted by invitation (`chainFor`), and A's copy had none.
- No `cluster-tx:read-repair-deferred` line appeared in any of the 3 debug runs. The deferral the old ticket measured is real, but it was not what kept P off A's list.
- Experiment: the edits above, plus the open reactions moved from `connection:open` to `peer:identify` filtered on the control protocol prefix (`/optimystic/${controlNetworkName()}`). 2 of 2 runs passed; A listed P after 12.27 s and 12.21 s, the reconnect reload's bound. There were no deferrals and no solo commit by A after it restarted. A's first-growth re-touch now went to the cohort, conflicted with M's revision, and finished after about 7.4 s of upstream retries: the existing "known benign race", now on the first write rather than a second one.

## Expected

A machine counts a sibling as reachable only once that sibling is in the cohort Optimystic would assemble: connected, identified with a protocol under the control network's prefix in its peer-store record, and accepted by `isNetworkMember` (an authorized member or a device being admitted). Everything that today means "connected" for control writes uses that one predicate:

- The growth edge (the write-while-alone drain) fires on the first such sibling, not the first connection of any kind, and re-arms when the last such sibling's connections have all closed.
- The sibling-open reactions in `onControlSiblingConnectionChange` (view drops, the 11 s reload timer, the reconcile trigger) fire on that sibling's identify, once per connected period. libp2p re-emits `peer:identify` on every identify-push from a connected peer, and those repeats must not re-drop views or start passes.
- `committedAlone()` is true when no such sibling exists. A write made with only a relay, a bootstrap node, or a not-yet-identified peer connected is then queued and re-issued on the growth edge, as the "Writes made while alone" section says it should be.
- `publishFirstSelfRecord`'s connected-only condition and the revoked-row reap gate (`runReconcileControlCohort`, "the fork this gate exists to avoid") use the same predicate.

The public `getControlConnectionCount()` is an embedder's report of replication reach (the admin channel's `controlConnections` / `alone` fields). Its doc says `committedAlone` is defined in terms of it, which stops being true. Recommended default: keep the raw count for `controlConnections`, and add a public read of the new predicate for `alone`, so the admin channel's warning matches what the write path does. Update the spec "agrees with the write-while-alone seam" to agree with the new predicate.

Cost: the reactions move to a later event; they are not added. A reconnect where nothing changed must cost what it costs today. Record the `cadre` and `invite` cases of `message-cost-accounting.integration.ts` before and after.

## Not covered here

- **Consults Optimystic defers.** A held block whose consult is deferred (16 already in flight) starts nothing and stays unstamped, so the next storage read starts it. With the view's maximum age at 15 s, that read is the first view read more than 15 s later. On a node with no activity, that is the 2-minute reconcile backstop: the staleness bound "Membership rows view" → "Maximum age" already states for a change no event announces. This is a tripwire, not a defect. Park it as a `NOTE:` at `armMembershipViewReload`: if `read-repair-deferred` lines show at a reconnect, re-arm the reload once more instead of waiting for the backstop.
- **Upstream (Optimystic, read only here).** Two upstream gaps let one solo write do lasting damage. (1) A consult and a catch-up push treat equal revision numbers as equal content, so a same-revision fork is invisible to both. (2) A commit takes the solo path while a connected peer that will serve the network has not yet identified; `bootstrapContactImminent` waits only for configured bootstrap peers. The cadre-side change removes the trigger seen here. These two are for a human to raise upstream as defence in depth; nothing here waits on them.
