# Doc-writing rule, and the control-write retry bullet as its first application

First of three tickets. This one states the rule and restructures one bullet as the worked example; `architecture-doc-convergence-status-block-to-sections` and `architecture-doc-remaining-long-bullets` apply the same rule to the rest.

## Why a rule and not just a split

Each ticket that touches a subsystem appends its story to the bullet that already covers it, and nothing is removed because everything was true when written. The control-write retry bullet grew from 1412 to 1682 words in one ticket (2026-09-17) and measures 1886 words on 2026-09-30. Splitting it without a written rule means it re-merges.

Measure with (one paragraph per line is the repo convention, so a line is a bullet or a paragraph):

```bash
awk '{n=split($0,a,/ /); if (n>500) printf "%d\t%d words\n", NR, n}' docs/architecture.md
```

On 2026-09-30 this reports twelve lines: 101 (1886), 234 (1066), 235 (1840), 237 (928), 410 (619), 411 (883), 428 (500), 571 (650), 572 (797), 802 (622), 804 (515), 1170 (659). Line numbers move as other tickets edit the file; find a bullet by its bold opening words.

## The rule (text to add)

**`AGENTS.md`**, under "General", directly after the bullet "Don't create summary docs; update existing docs" — three bullets in that file's terse style:

- Docs describe the system as it is now. A problem that was found and fixed is history: name the ticket slug (or the upstream commit) and stop. Do not retell the diagnosis, the dates, or the before-state. Keep a past fact only when it is the reason the current design is the way it is, and then state it as a constraint, not a story.
- One list item, one claim plus its reason. When an item needs a second topic, give it a labelled sub-paragraph; when it describes a mechanism with several parts, make it a headed section and leave a one-sentence item linking to it.
- Extending a doc: add to the section that owns the topic, or add a section. Do not append to the nearest bullet.

**`docs/architecture.md`**, one short paragraph directly after the opening sentence (before `## Overview`), saying: this document describes current behaviour; the record of how a behaviour was arrived at lives in `tickets/complete/`, and for tickets already swept from there, in `tickets/.pruned-tickets.jsonl` (slug → landing commit, readable with `git show <commit>`); recurring test-failure history lives in `tickets/.pre-existing-known.md`; authoring rules are in `AGENTS.md`. Do not restate the three rules there.

No word cap and no lint rule — both were considered in the plan ticket and declined: some reasoning needs a long paragraph, and a cap makes authors split mid-thought.

## The two structural forms

These are the two forms the rule's second bullet names. All three tickets use them and nothing else.

**Labelled sub-paragraphs** — for an item that stays in its list. The item keeps its bold opening claim and first paragraph; each further topic becomes its own paragraph, indented to the list item (two spaces for a top-level item), separated by a blank line, opening with a short bold run-in label ending in a period. The document already does this at the "Every node on the same network should use the same value" group under "Replication cluster size" (`**When it takes effect.**`).

**Headed section** — for an item that is really a mechanism. The content moves to a new heading one level below the section that owns it; the list keeps a one- or two-sentence item stating the claim and linking to the new heading.
