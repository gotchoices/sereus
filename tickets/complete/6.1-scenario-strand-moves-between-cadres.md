# Complete: scenario — an app's strands move between cadres

## What was built

`packages/integration-tests/src/scenarios/strand-moves-between-cadres.integration.ts`, one test, the real-network proof for `exportStrandBundle` / `adoptStrandBundle`. Machines: **N** (the app's one node, one identity key, `transaction`), **P** (an external party, `storage`, its own owner), **O** (party A's owner, `listenAddrs: []`), **M** (A's always-on member, `storage`, `hostUnclaimedStrands`), **Q** (a stranger party, added in step 5).

1. **In B.** N founds closed strand S and publishes a formation invitation bound to it with two uses; P redeems it and its Member row is seated. P founds open strand T and N joins it; the scenario reads that T is a machine-local join on N and that B's `JoinedStrand` table is empty. One row crosses each way on each strand. N then refuses P's S strand node (a `peerDialGate` with `inbound: true` on N, plus `hangUp`), waits until P has left N's strand cohort, writes one row to S alone, and reads that P does not have it.
2. **Party A.** O founds A, admits M (`startPinningMember` + `admitMember`), mints an owner-granting invitation for N's peer id, waits until M holds its row, stops, and waits until it has left M's control cohort (the `cadre-invite-any-member` pattern).
3. **Export and switch.** `exportStrandBundle(N)` is read entry by entry: `[S, own]` then `[T, joined]`; S's row, a non-null party key equal to B's `StrandPartyKey`, S's saved routing table present; T's row with no party key and non-empty carried addresses; one formation invitation, `usesLeft: 1`; no pending joins. N stops; a node with the same key, the same raw-storage capture and the same control port starts for A (no genesis), redeems at M (`redeemWaitingForMember`), and wires its owner key.
4. **Adopt twice.** The report is `{ moved: [S, T], refused: [], failed: [], invitationsMoved: 1, invitationsDropped: 0, pendingJoinsMoved: 0 }`, and the second adoption returns the same report. A holds one `Strand` row for S (founder column = N's owner key), one `JoinedStrand` row for T, one `FormationInvite` row for the token, and S's party key equal to the bundle's.
5. **The strands run in A.** N claims S and T from `strand:discovered`. N's strand peer for S is the one P knew. P's S strand node reconnects with N and receives the row written alone; P's new rows on S and T reach N; M launches a replica of each from A's rows and holds every block N holds (`awaitBlockCoverage`, floor of 4 blocks); Q redeems the S envelope handed out in B against N, gets a membership invitation, and A's `countFormationUsage(token)` reaches 1.
6. **Identity unchanged.** On P and on N: S's `Member` keys are exactly {N's member key from the moved party key, P's}, and `Manager` is exactly [N's member key]. On P, N's member key is bound to N's strand peer, and to no peer other than N's or M's replica.

**Measured (2026-10-11, Windows, loopback):** 4 of 4 runs passed, one of them as two runs in parallel. The test took 15.8–18.8 s; the file, transform and import included, 31.7–35.6 s. P left N's strand cohort 4.0–5.0 s after the cut. After the claim, P reconnected in 0.9–2.1 s, and the row written alone arrived 49–771 ms after that. N held 33 committed blocks for S and 9 for T. Logs: `tickets/.logs/scenario-strand-moves-between-cadres.run{1..4}.log`.

## Deviations from the ticket, and known gaps

- **N listens on loopback, not `listenAddrs: []`.** P and Q redeem formation invitations by dialing N. The ticket rules out a relay-only link, and with no listen address and no relay nothing can dial N. `strand-formation-cross-party-seed`, the scenario the ticket points to, uses a loopback listener. N's control node restarts on the same port (`sameListenAddr`), which stands in for an unchanged relay address. Its strand nodes come back on new ephemeral ports.
- **N's reconcile timer is parked in B** (`RECONCILE_PARKED_MS`). Any control connection counts as "connected" for the pass that publishes joins, and N holds one to P's node after the formations, so a timed pass could publish T into B's own `JoinedStrand` table. The export reads that table too, but the ticket asks for the machine-local shape. A `NOTE:` at the constant names the one other trigger (`self:peer:update` on an address change). The always-on replica scenario has the same residual.
- **The scenario does not check which side dials first after the switch.** The header says only that P's addresses for N's strand nodes go stale and that N's rebuilt nodes start from the routing tables the bundle carried. Asserting the connection's direction could flake: M's replica could forward N's new address record to P first.
- **The scenario allows M's replica peer under N's member key on P.** In every run P bound two peers to N's member key, N's and M's: once M is in N's party, its replica registers itself as a device of that party's member. The assertion requires N's peer and allows M's. A reviewer may want this pinned as "exactly both", but whether a storage replica writes the binding at all is not this ticket's subject.
- **Q is checked only to the formation**: `formStrand` result and A's usage count. Q never runs S, so Q's Member row is not seated. That keeps step 6's exact member set clean.
- **Step 6 reads S's membership on P without a wait.** The site comment says why: the founding rows reached P before P's own Member row, which step 1 waited for. `strand-formation-cross-party-seed` makes the same read.
- **Two "CadreNode has no keyStore" warnings** in the output come from P and Q, which join strands from another party on memory-only stores. They are harmless and match the other formation scenarios.
- **Not run:** the full integration suite. The harness changes are behaviour-preserving for existing callers: the default member options are unchanged, and `control()` behaves the same for a one-party capture. Their only other users are the `cadre-invite-*`, `app-joins-cadre-by-invitation`, `owner-anchor-follows-owner-changes`, `control-offline-read-after-restart` and `cadre-invite-row-unreplicated` scenarios. Typecheck and lint cover the signatures; I did not rerun those scenarios.

## Review findings

Read the diff of `ticket(implement): scenario-strand-moves-between-cadres` first, then the handoff, then the original implement ticket.

- **Correctness, fixed inline — a false premise in docs and comments.** `docs/strands.md` (Publication), `docs/architecture.md` (Moving the app's strands), `strand-move.ts` (`exportStrands`), `CadreNode.listUnpublishedJoinedStrands` and `JoinedStrandSession.unpublishedRecords` all said a one-node party never holds a control connection and so never publishes its joins. The reconcile pass gates publication on `getControlConnectionCount() > 0`, which counts any connection on the control node (a relay's, or another party's node after a formation), so a one-node app party with cross-party strands does publish. That is why the implementer had to park N's reconcile timer. All five texts now say joins are machine-local until the machine holds a control connection and party-wide rows after, and that the export reads both. The scenario's two comments that repeated the claim now say the parked timer is what keeps T machine-local. No behaviour change: the export already unions both sources.
- **Harness changes:** checked. `startPinningMember` keeps `strandFilter: 'none'` by default. `captureRawStorage().control()` now refuses a second control scope; every other caller (`cadre-invite-row-unreplicated`, `control-offline-read-after-restart`) drives one party through its capture, so their behaviour is unchanged. The removed `NOTE:` asked for exactly this change.
- **Tests:** the scenario is the only real-network proof of the move and stays. The new `block-store-probe.spec.ts` case pins the new refusal branch, at the same level as the spec's other refusal cases, and stays. Nothing cut: no test restates the implementation or verifies a mock.
- **Scenario assertions against the original ticket:** every step is covered. Accepted as the handoff describes: N listens on loopback (dialable formations need it), the dial direction after the switch is not asserted, and M's replica peer is allowed under N's member key on P (a party's machines bind as devices of one member, which `docs/testing.md` already covers in another scenario). Step 6's immediate reads are sound for the reason given at the site.
- **Parked reconcile timer:** the shape it forces (T machine-local in B) is one of the two production shapes, and both go through the same export loop. The existing `NOTE:` at `RECONCILE_PARKED_MS` already records the one other trigger. No new tripwire.
