# A `CadrePeer` row's two writers refuse each other, and the loser is now re-run

Landed in `ticket(implement): cadre-peer-lost-update-is-not-re-run`; this review pass made comment, doc and ticket-board corrections only (see *Review findings*). The superseded backlog ticket `bug-owner-write-racing-a-machines-own-publish-fails` was deleted by the implement stage.

## What landed

**The policy.** `control-write-retry.ts` ships a third policy, `LOST_UPDATE_RETRY_POLICY`: the default classifier plus `ConcurrentModificationError` claimed by type, over the default attempts, backoff and 10 s budget. `matchesRetriableFailure` takes an optional link predicate that runs beside the final-torn-write claim and after both vetoes, so a lost update carried inside `PartialCommitError`, `CoordinatorPartialCommitError`, `SyncRetryExhaustedError` or a non-final `TornActionError` stays declined. The default list is untouched. `mutateCadrePeer` takes an optional policy it passes to `lockedWithRetry`.

**Self-publish decides inside the body.** `ControlDatabase.updateSelfPeerRecord(peerId, sign)` runs under the policy. Its body reads the row with the read retry off (`queryPeerRecord` gained a `retry` flag, threaded through a new `findCadrePeerRow`), returns `null` without writing when the row is absent or its stamp is retired, otherwise calls the signer over the row just read and writes. It returns the record written. `CadreNode.publishSelfRecord`'s update branch is one call with `signSelfRecord` as the signer.

**Owner re-touch decides inside the body.** `reauthorizeCadrePeer(peerId, mode, ownerKey, sign)` has two modes and reports `'retouched' | 'absent' | 'self-published'`. `'rebroadcast'` (the write-while-alone drain) bumps `UpdatedAt` to `max(now, current + 1)` from the row read inside the body and writes only while `Sig` is null. `'revouch'` (`removeOwner`) writes regardless and leaves `UpdatedAt` out of the `set` list. `retouchTarget` is the one decision function for the pre-check outside the lock (which keeps the absent and self-published cases from taking the lock or notifying) and the re-check inside it. `SeedBootstrapService.reauthorizePeer(peerId, mode, sign?)` passes the mode through. The drain clears its entry on every reported outcome; the reconstruct sweep skips rows it already holds as self-published and counts only `'retouched'`.

**Removal opts in.** `deleteCadrePeer` and `removeMachine` pass the policy through `mutateCadrePeer`; their bodies already read the stamp and sign inside.

**Docs.** `docs/architecture.md` → "Control-write retry" names the lost-update class, the opt-in rule and sites, the measured retry ceiling, and why a lost update is re-decided rather than re-presented.

## Review findings

**Read first:** the implement commit's full diff, with the lock, retry and view-read code around it (`withWriteLock`, `runWriteBody`, `lockedWithRetry`, `readMembershipRows`, `deleteGuardedRow`, `removeMachine`, `publishSelfRecord`, the drain guards), then the handoff.

**Correctness, checked and found sound (no change):**
- The retry wraps the lock, so the backoff sleep runs with no lock held; the two new specs that stage a rival write in the injected sleep rely on exactly that, and it holds.
- Every opted-in body reads, decides and signs inside the lock: the self-publish through `findCadrePeerRow` with retry off; the re-touch through the same read plus `retouchTarget`; the removals through `deleteGuardedRow`'s in-body stamp read and `removeMachine`'s in-body `signMachineRemovals` / `signWithdrawals`. The invitation keys the removal withdraws come from the caller's plan, which a self-publish does not touch.
- A lost update inside a multi-collection transaction (`removeMachine`) after a sibling tree synced arrives as a partial commit and the possibly-stored veto declines it by type before the new claim runs; the retry spec pins both wrappers.
- The in-body read of a stale membership view cannot produce a wrong write: the engine's own read set decides the lost update, and a stale `UpdatedAt` under the clock is dominated by `Date.now()`; one ahead of the clock is refused by the schema's monotonic rule, unretried, as before.
- `reissuePeerAuthorize` now throws instead of returning when the seed service is missing; both callers are gated on `canAuthorize()` or the service's presence before they reach it, so the throw is a defensive guard, not a behaviour change.
- The hub spec's "does not notify when the removal target is already absent" still holds through the pre-check outside the lock.
- The revoked-node skip survives: a revoked node's outside read returns null, the insert path no-ops on the physically present row, and the body's retired-stamp check returns `null` so nothing is refreshed. This is also why the in-body `Revocation` read cannot simply move outside (recorded in the new `NOTE:` below).

**Tripwires recorded:**
- `NOTE:` in `ControlDatabase.updateSelfPeerRecord`'s body: the in-body view reads are charged to the retry budget, so a cold view load that waits out a per-peer read deadline leaves the attempt no room for its one retry. Normally a cache hit because the caller's outside read warms the views; the racing case is where a table change drops them. Says what to measure first if the residual matters and why the retired-stamp check cannot move outside.
- The residual abandonment rate (about 1 boot in 18 in the degraded-cohort harness, where one member cannot reach one sibling) is recorded as a second arm on `backlog/debt-degraded-member-scenario-sometimes-loses-the-conflict-race`, the open ticket that already owns that file's flakiness, with the three decisions it leaves to a human (bound the re-run differently, make the losing attempt's reads cheaper, or make the `afterEach` check treat a budget abandonment of a boot-time self-publish as the known residual). Not a new ticket: it is conditional on that rate mattering, and the production effect is a delayed address record, redone by the next reconcile pass or heartbeat, not a lost one.
- The spurious membership notify when the re-touch's in-body re-check reaches a no-write outcome (`'absent'` or `'self-published'` after losing a race) is documented on `reauthorizeCadrePeer` and left alone: the pre-check keeps the common case quiet, and a notify only re-reads the member set. Folding a wrote-or-not predicate into `mutateCadrePeer` for it would cost more code than the extra read it saves.
