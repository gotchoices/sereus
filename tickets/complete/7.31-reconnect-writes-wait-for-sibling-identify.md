# A reconnecting machine waits for its sibling to identify before writing as connected

Implemented in `ticket(implement): reconnect-writes-wait-for-sibling-identify`; reviewed in this pass.

## What was wrong

`CadreNode` treated "a control connection is open" as "my writes reach the cohort". Optimystic admits a peer into a control block's cohort only once the peer's identify has filled its peer-store protocol list with the control network's `repo` or `cluster` protocol, and only if the control node's `isNetworkMember` accepts it. Between `connection:open` and that identify (tens of milliseconds to about a second) every control write commits on a cohort of one. A restarted owner's first-growth drain re-touched its own `CadrePeer` row in that gap; at an equal revision number with the sibling's copy, that was a fork no consult or peer-join push sees, and the owner never listed machines admitted while it was down (`cadre-invite-any-member` with the follow-on edits failed 3 of 4).

## What changed

- **One predicate, `CadreNode.hasReachableControlSibling()` (public).** True when some peer in `identifiedControlPeers` passes `isControlCohortMember` (the stream gate's member rule plus devices being admitted — the same function now wired as Optimystic's `isNetworkMember`). A peer enters `identifiedControlPeers` on its first `peer:identify` of a connected period whose protocols name the control database (`servesControlDatabase`: `/optimystic/control-<party>/repo/1.0.0` or `/cluster/1.0.0`, Optimystic's own `servesThisNetwork` test). It leaves when its last connection closes. Membership is applied at read time, so a sibling that identified before it was authorized (connect-then-authorize) or that is later revoked is judged by the current snapshot.
- **Growth edge** (`updateControlSiblingReachability`): false→true drains the write-while-alone queue; true→false re-arms. Evaluated on identify, on connection close, and at the end of every membership refresh (`refreshAuthorizedControlPeers`). Not on `admittingDevices` changes, deliberately (comment says why).
- **Sibling-open reactions** (view drops, the 11 s reload, the reconcile trigger) moved from `connection:open` to the identify that first counts the peer; identify-push repeats do nothing. The close reaction still runs on every `connection:close`. The `connection:open` listener is gone.
- **Peers identified before the listeners were wired** (`noteIdentifiedControlPeers`, from the peer store at wire time) are counted and update the edge, but get no open reaction, matching what a pre-wiring connection got before.
- `committedAlone()`, `publishFirstSelfRecord`'s guard and the revoked-row reap gate (plus the joined-strand sync behind it) all read the predicate.
- `getControlConnectionCount()` stays the raw count (admin `controlConnections`); admin `alone` now reads `!hasReachableControlSibling()`.
- NOTE added at `armMembershipViewReload` for deferred consults (tripwire from the ticket). The two NOTEs that pointed at the replaced fix ticket now point at `implement/invited-machine-skips-its-first-publish`.
- Docs: "Writes made while alone" rewritten around the predicate with a "Why identify, not a connection" paragraph; "After a sibling connects" fires on identify, with a "When a consult is deferred" paragraph replacing the old fix-ticket pointer; other mentions of "no control connection" / "0→≥1 edge" updated in architecture.md, strands.md and doc comments.
- Noted on `backlog/bug-seed-connection-counted-as-a-control-connection` that this change removes its suspected path (a placeholder node serves another party's protocols); only its confirmation step remains.

## Tests

- `cadre-node-control-replication.spec.ts` → "connection-growth transition", now driven through the real listeners with dispatched libp2p events:
  - "drains on the first identified sibling only, and re-arms once the last one has gone" — identify (not open) fires the drain once; an identify-push and a second sibling do not; it re-arms only when the last sibling's connection closes.
  - "an open with no identify serving the control database neither drains nor counts as reachable" (new) — an unidentified connection and a relay that identified with non-control protocols: counted by `getControlConnectionCount`, not by `hasReachableControlSibling`, no drain.

  - The `inject` helper now marks each fake connection as an identified sibling; `cadre-node-control-cohort.spec.ts`'s `injectCohort` does the same from its `connections` list.

## Known gaps and judgment calls (from the implement handoff)

- **Formation costs more streams.** The drain's re-issues now actually reach the cohort, which is their purpose, but the ticket's "cost must not rise" line was written about reconnects. Decide whether the invite delta needs a second look.
- **Reconnect within one process is slightly conservative.** libp2p's in-memory peer store keeps a sibling's protocols after it disconnects, so Optimystic counts a returning sibling at `connection:open`, while cadre waits for the new identify. A write in that gap is queued and re-issued (an idempotent re-touch). Not measured.
- **`noteIdentifiedControlPeers` has no unit test.** It covers connections that identified during `start()` before the listeners were wired; before this change such a connection fired nothing until another connection opened, so this is new behaviour (the edge now updates) with no open reaction.
- **Connect-then-authorize has no dedicated test.** The refresh hook is one line; the invitation and claim scenarios exercise that join order end to end but none asserts the drain fires there.
- **Admitting devices don't trigger the edge.** `hasReachableControlSibling` counts them (as Optimystic does), so a write during an admission is not queued, but the drain waits for the device's row to land and refresh membership. Comment at `updateControlSiblingReachability`.
- **Upstream defence in depth stays open**, for a human to raise with Optimystic: equal revision numbers are treated as equal content by consults and catch-up pushes, and a commit takes the solo path while a connected serving peer has not identified yet.
- Logs from these runs are in `tickets/.logs/reconnect-identify.*` (pruned automatically).

## Review findings

Read the diff of `ticket(implement): reconnect-writes-wait-for-sibling-identify` before the handoff, then checked the libp2p and Optimystic code the change depends on (`node_modules/@optimystic/db-p2p/node_modules/libp2p` 3.3.11, `@libp2p/identify` 4.1.14).

**Correctness: checked, no defects found.**
- `peer:identify` reaches the predicate on every connection, relayed ones included: db-p2p runs identify itself on each connection open (`network/identify-on-open.js`), and `@libp2p/identify`'s `consumeIdentifyMessage` dispatches `peer:identify` for both the initial identify and every identify-push. So a sibling reachable only over a limited relay circuit still becomes reachable; the predicate does not leave such a node queued forever.
- A sibling whose control handlers register after its libp2p start identifies first without the control protocols and then pushes them; the handler ignores the first and counts the peer on the push, because only a serving identify adds to `identifiedControlPeers` and none removes.
- Close ordering: libp2p dispatches each event on its internal bus (where the connection manager drops the closed connection) before re-dispatching it to the node's listeners, so `getConnections(peer)` in `onControlConnectionClosed` already excludes the closed connection. The code's reason ("it subscribed first") was wrong although the conclusion holds; comment corrected.
- Membership applied at read time: revocation through `refreshAuthorizedControlPeers` re-evaluates the edge; the bootstrap-mode rule (empty authorized set admits any peer) matches Optimystic's `isNetworkMember`, and `servesControlDatabase` is party-specific, so another party's control node never counts.
- Every former reader of the raw count that meant "will this write travel" now reads the predicate (`committedAlone` and through it `ownerKeyWriteAlone`, the pending-join runner's `isAlone`, the reap gate, `publishFirstSelfRecord`, admin `alone`). The remaining raw `getConnections().length === 0` check (`strandAddrRefreshTargets`) is a read shortcut, not a write gate, and is correct as is.
- `stop()` clears `identifiedControlPeers` and the edge; `noteIdentifiedControlPeers` guards against a stop while its peer-store reads are out.

**Message cost: accepted as measured.** The extra formation streams are the drain's re-issues reaching the cohort, which before this change committed alone and left the reader to pull the row (the cadre case's 547 ms list phase dropped to 79 ms, and gets fell). The invite case's +8/+3 `cluster` streams are within what the handoff measured as run-to-run spread for formation, and idle cost is unchanged. The ticket's "cost must not rise" was about reconnects; the instrument has no reconnect case, so that stays argued rather than measured. Not filed: no current-release anchor needs a reconnect case added to `message-cost-accounting` beyond what this ticket argued.

**Docs: updated by the implementer, one leftover fixed.** Read architecture.md ("Writes made while alone", "After a sibling connects", "When a consult is deferred", the replication-breadth item, owner-key refusals), strands.md and the touched doc comments; they describe the predicate. `control-write-while-alone-convergence.integration.ts`'s file header still said "0→≥1 control-connection growth edge"; reworded to the reachable-sibling edge.

**Tests: kept as written, none added or cut.** The two growth-transition tests drive the real listeners with dispatched libp2p events and each pins one behaviour (identify, not open, drains once and re-arms on the last close; a non-serving or unidentified connection is counted by `getControlConnectionCount` and not by the predicate). The admin `alone` test pins the field reading the predicate, not the count. Gaps the handoff named remain gaps by choice: `noteIdentifiedControlPeers` (pre-wiring peers) and the connect-then-authorize refresh hook have no unit test; both are one-step glue, and the hook is exercised end to end by the invitation and claim scenarios. Not worth a fake `refreshAuthorizedControlPeers` harness.

**Judgment calls from the handoff: left as designed.**
- Conservative in-process reconnect (Optimystic may count a returning sibling from its peer-store record at `connection:open`, cadre waits for the new identify): a write in that gap is queued and re-issued idempotently. Harmless.
- Admitting devices count in the predicate but do not drive the edge; the drain waits for the device's row to land and refresh membership. The comment at `updateControlSiblingReachability` says why.
- Upstream defence in depth (equal revision numbers treated as equal content; a commit taking the solo path while a connected serving peer has not identified) is Optimystic's to change; sibling repositories are read-only here, so it stays with the human, as the handoff says.

**Size and hygiene.** `cadre-node.ts` grew by ~70 lines net in a file already far past any size bar; the new logic is in small single-purpose methods and does not make the split harder. No new size ticket: none would change what the next ticket touches.
