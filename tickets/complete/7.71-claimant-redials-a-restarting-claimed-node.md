# The claimant redials the claimed node while it restarts

## What was built

Owner side of a QR claim (`CadreNode.claimNode`), after node B acknowledges the claim seed:

- **Claim address first.** Right after the ack, before anything else is awaited, `connectedAddrFirst` moves the QR address that matches the open connection's `remoteAddr` (compared through `addrKey`, which ignores a trailing `/p2p/<peerId>`) to the front of the list given to `retainDialTarget`. If the connection has already closed, or the QR address is a DNS name, the QR order is kept.
- **Redial window.** `claimNode` starts `redialClaimedNode(peerId)` (not awaited, failures logged). Every `CLAIM_RESTART_REDIAL_STEP_MS` (1 s) for `CONTROL_COHORT_REDIAL_MS` (15 s), a step stops if the node has stopped, the retained target is gone (`removePeer`), or B has identified as serving this party's control database (`identifiedControlPeers`); otherwise it dials once through `dialBootstrapPeer(..., 'claimNode(redial)')` when no connection to B is open. A dial that connects does not end the loop, because a restarting node's bring-up quiet period lets the dialer's dial resolve and then drops the connection. One loop per peer; the reconcile redial ladder is left alone.
- **Accepted-tradeoff `NOTE:`** at the `vouchPeer` call: B's signed record is not carried in the ack (it would be signed before the restart, and the write-while-alone drain re-sends only unsigned rows).
- `dialBootstrapPeer` takes a log `context`; the cold-start dial label is now `reconcileControlCohort(cold-start) dial of bootstrap peer X`.
- The backlog bug `bug-seed-connection-counted-as-a-control-connection` was confirmed not a bug (debug logs of `node-claim-by-phone` show the drain starts only once the restarted B identifies) and deleted; the one backlog line that cited it was updated.

Measured by the implementer with temporary overrides (claimant backstop 120 s, 2 s sleep before the claim): reconnect after `claimNode` 0.25–2.08 s with the loop, versus 0.28 s or ~5.5 s without it. The ~5.5 s reconnect without the loop came from a dial below cadre-core (no cadre-core dial log line), not from the 15 s ladder step; not investigated.

## Review findings

Read the `ticket(implement): claimant-redials-a-restarting-claimed-node` diff first, then the handoff.

**Correctness — checked, no defects found.**
- `connectedAddrFirst` reads `this.controlNode.getConnections`; confirmed the seed service is installed on the same control node (`installSeedBootstrapService(..., this.controlNode, ...)`), so the seed connection is the one it sees. A connection already closed by the restart degrades to the QR order, which the docstring states.
- `retainDialTarget` makes the entry visible synchronously (store contract), so the loop's first step finds it. `bootstrapPeerStore.all()` is an in-memory copy, safe to read after `stop()`; the step checks `_running` and ends within one step (≤ 1 s) of a stop, and the timer is unref'd.
- Stop rule: `identifiedControlPeers` is filled only by a `peer:identify` listing this party's control protocols, which the placeholder node never does; it is cleared on stop. A repeat claim with B already identified ends at its first step (scenario step 5 passes).
- An empty `multiaddrs` claim retains no target, so the loop ends at its first step.
- The loop's step dial and a reconcile pass's dial of the same peer can overlap; libp2p joins dials to one peer, and the step's result is only logged.

**Interaction found — recorded as a tripwire.** A connection close to an authorized sibling starts a reconcile pass at once, and a sibling in its bring-up quiet period lets the dial resolve and then drops it, so a restarting sibling (any restart, not only a claim) costs back-to-back dial-connect-close passes for its whole bring-up (5–6 in ~200 ms locally). Bounded by bring-up time today. Parked as a `NOTE:` at `onControlSiblingConnectionChange` in `cadre-node.ts` with its revisit condition (a slow device's bring-up making the churn visible).

**Tests.** No test added or cut. The redial loop has no automated coverage: `node-claim-by-phone`'s phone fixture caps the ladder at 2 s, so the loop's benefit is invisible there, and a timing assertion against a default-backstop claimant would have less than a 2× margin against the ~5.5 s lower-layer reconnect (implementer's measurement). A unit test would have to mock `CadreNode`'s own connection state, which is the wrong layer. The address reorder is not exercised (every scenario's QR code has one loopback address); checked by reading.
