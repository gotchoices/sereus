# Formation deadlines derived from the declared link

Implemented in `ticket(implement): formation-deadlines-derived-from-the-link`; reviewed here. The review moved the derivation into its own module and recorded one tripwire; everything else in the implement handoff stands.

## What changed

Strand formation is the protocol one party's machine runs to join another party's strand: the joiner (initiator) dials the host's control node (responder), sends one contact frame, and waits for one result frame. Every deadline on it was a fixed number chosen on a fast network. They are now all computed by one function, `formationDeadlines(linkRoundTripMs)` in `strand-formation-deadlines.ts`, from the machine's declared link round trip (`NetworkConfig.linkRoundTripMs`, default 3 500 ms), using the counts in `link-budget.ts`. With `L` the declaration:

| field | formula | at the default (L = 3 500) | at L = 100 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| dialMs (initiator: open the connection, negotiate the protocol) | 5L + 2 000 | 19 500 | 2 500 |
| awaitContactMs (responder: the contact-frame read) | 2L | 7 000 | 200 |
| validationMs (responder: the token and disclosure reads, counted, not wrapped) | 4L | 14 000 | 400 |
| provisionWorkMs (responder: hook work budget, then its signal aborts) | 10 000 + 24L | 94 000 | 12 400 |
| provisionGraceMs (responder: settle grace, contains one seat read plus one commit) | 22L | 77 000 | 2 200 |
| responseTravelMarginMs (what the initiator's wait holds over the responder's) | 5L | 17 500 | 500 |
| initiatorAwaitResponseMs (initiator: the result-frame read) | 10 000 + 51L | 188 500 | 15 100 |
| sessionMs (both roles) | 12 000 + 56L | 208 000 | 17 600 |

Two new counts in `link-budget.ts`: `PROTOCOL_NEGOTIATION_ROUND_TRIPS = 1` (and `CIRCUIT_REQUEST_ROUND_TRIPS` is now expressed as that plus one) and `COMMIT_ROUND_TRIPS = 20`, a measurement rather than a count. The measurement's figures (optimystic `9e5c1e85`, 2026-09-23, the `delayed` configuration of `relay-round-trip-measure.integration.ts`: one insert took 3.8 to 6.1 s at a 300 ms link round trip, up to 20.3 round trips) are copied into the constant's doc comment, which is now their home, with the instruction to re-measure rather than recompute when `@optimystic/*` is bumped. Two helpers: `relayedStreamOpenBudgetMs` (a relayed dial plus one negotiation, 19 500 ms at the default) and `commitBudgetMs` (70 000 ms at the default).

The dial now runs under `withDeadline`, so an expired dial is cancelled through the signal handed to `dialProtocol`; a stream that arrives after the signal aborted (the dial settled in the same tick the deadline fired) is reset rather than leaked. `stepTimeoutMs` is gone from the initiator's options and replaced by `dialTimeoutMs`; on the listener it still exists and now means the contact-frame read only. Both roles take `linkRoundTripMs`, and `StrandFormationManagerConfig` gains `linkRoundTripMs` and `dialTimeoutMs`. `CadreNode.initializeStrandSolicitation` fills `formationConfig.linkRoundTripMs` from the node's network config unless the caller's formation config already carries one. The approval hook's timeout is exported from `formation-approval.ts` as `DEFAULT_APPROVAL_TIMEOUT_MS`, with its `link-independent` lint reason, and imported by the derivation; no module cycle (the control-database subtree never reaches the protocol). The three retired constants and their four `link-bound, not yet derived` lint directives are gone.

## Two departures from the plan ticket, kept

The plan said the responder's clamp reserve "becomes the derived travel margin". With per-role rooms that is not enough: the initiator's clamped wait is `session - dial`, the responder's reply lands `margin` after its provisioning ends, so provisioning must end by `session - dial - margin`, and the responder's own room is `session - awaitContact - validation`. The reserve is the difference (`responderClampReserveMs`: 16 000 ms at the default, 2 400 at L = 100), floored at zero and capped at half the room. The review checked this by algebra: with the reserve, the responder's clamped budget plus the margin equals the initiator's clamped wait exactly, and the unclamped defaults have the same equality by construction.

The clamp triggers on `requested > ceiling`, not `>=`, because the derived defaults sit exactly at the ceiling (the responder's ceiling with the reserve above reduces to `provisionWorkMs + provisionGraceMs` at every link; the reserve is always under half the room since `2 000 + 4L < 10 000 + 46L`), so `>=` logged a spurious clamp warning on every listener construction.

## Review findings

Read first, with fresh eyes, the whole diff of `ticket(implement): formation-deadlines-derived-from-the-link`; then the current `strand-formation-protocol.ts`, `control-stream.ts` (`withTimeout`, `withDeadline`, `exchangeFrame`), the responder's provisioning path (`StrandFormationManager.provisionAsResponder`, `ControlFormationUsageRecorder`, `ControlDatabase.recordFormationUsage` and `redeemInvitation`, `CadreNode.issueStrandMembershipInvite`), libp2p's `dial-queue.js`, and every doc the change touched.

**Checked and found correct (no change):**

- The late-stream reset in `openFormationStream`: `withTimeout` runs `onTimeout` (which aborts the signal) before it rejects, and the `.then` attached inside the op is queued ahead of `withTimeout`'s own, so a dial that resolves after the deadline sees `signal.aborted` and resets the stream. A dial that resolves before the timer cannot be overtaken by it: the timer is a macrotask and both handlers are microtasks.
- libp2p's dial queue uses the caller's signal in place of its own `dialTimeout` (`options.signal ?? AbortSignal.timeout(this.dialTimeout)`), so the connection open is bounded by the 19.5 s formation dial rather than the 16 s connection-manager limit. That is what `docs/architecture.md` already says a caller's own deadline does, and the budget is the dial count plus one negotiation, the same shape as the one-frame control exchanges.
- The reserve derivation and the `>` clamp change, by algebra (above).
- The grace split never starves work: the derived grace is `22L` against a budget of `10 000 + 46L`, so the half-cap never bites at the defaults.
- Nothing outside cadre-core reads `stepTimeoutMs` on the dial side or the three retired constants; the only `formationConfig` callers are `CadreNode` and the e2e scenario's Phase 6, whose `provisionTimeoutMs: 3000` now merges with the node's link declaration as intended. Whole-workspace `yarn typecheck` is clean.
- The e2e scenario `strand-formation-e2e` (22 tests, including both Phase 6 arms) passes against a fresh cadre-core build, which the implement handoff had not run.

**Minor, fixed inline:**

- The derivation, its clamp helpers and the split (about 200 lines of pure arithmetic) lived in the protocol file, which had grown to 997 lines. Moved to `strand-formation-deadlines.ts`; the protocol file is 803 lines and imports four functions from it. `index.ts`, the manager, `formation-approval.ts`'s comment and the two docs were retargeted. The ordering test moved with it to `strand-formation-deadlines.spec.ts`.
- The comment on `formationDeadlines` described the work path as "a control read to resolve the host strand" and "the approval hook with its seat pre-check read", and validation as "two control reads". The code issues three control reads in validation (the invite twice, then the usage count) and about eight on the bound provisioning path before the `FormationUsage` write (invite and strand rows to resolve the host strand; invite, `ValidationKey` and seat count around the hook; strand row and `StrandPartyKey` to issue the membership invite; the host strand's stamp). The comment now says what runs and that `R` is charged as headroom for reads that consult the cohort, not per read, citing the consistency doc's steady-state statement that these reads touch held blocks. The numbers were deliberately not changed: counting every read would add about 50 s to the initiator's wait at the default declaration for a case (a cold responder) whose failure is a retryable "provisioning timed out" with the invite unspent, not a spent invite.
