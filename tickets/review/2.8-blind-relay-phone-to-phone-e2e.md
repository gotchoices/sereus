## What landed

**No product code changed.** The cross-party relay path — stranger-open formation over a
circuit, the formation-carried strand address, the closed-strand secret delivery, the
joiner's seeded auto-dial through the relay — worked end to end on the first run. What
landed is one new scenario, plus docs recording the proven shape and the measured cost.

**`packages/integration-tests/src/scenarios/blind-relay-phone-to-phone-e2e.integration.ts`**
— one phased test. Two DIFFERENT parties, each a single `CadreNode` with
`listenAddrs: []` and `relayAddrs: [<relay>]`, sharing the existing dedicated ungated
relay fixture (`harness/dedicated-relay.ts`, config-parity with `ops/docker/libp2p-infra`;
fixture unchanged). Asserts, in order:

- The relay speaks no `/sereus/strand-addr/1.0.0` (so delegate admission can only be a
  no-op — the announce fan-out folds unsupported-protocol failures to `[]`, and both
  strand launches resolving is the non-blocking proof).
- A boots relay-only (`profile: 'storage'`, `enableRelay: false` — a phone relays for
  nobody), every control addr is `/p2p-circuit`, reservation state `reserved`, relay
  count 1.
- A founds a CLOSED strand (`foundStrand`, type `'c'`, minted `MemberPrivateKey`) —
  live BEFORE the invitation exists; its strand node's configured-route reservation
  lands (circuit addr announced, relay count 2).
- The bound invitation (`createOpenInvitation` + `publishFormationInvite` with
  `strandId`, `totalUses: 1`) round-trips encode → decode (the out-of-band QR model),
  and every bootstrap address is a `/p2p-circuit` addr naming A's control peer.
- B (second party) boots relay-only the same way (relay count 3), decodes, and runs
  `formStrand` — the first-ever exercise of the stranger-open formation protocol
  (`STRANGER_OPEN_PROTOCOLS`) across a circuit. Result assertions: same `strandId`;
  `memberPrivateKey` EQUALS the founder's minted secret (closed-strand secret crossed
  the circuit); every carried `strandAddrs` entry is circuit-routed, names A's STRAND
  peer, is among A's live announced strand addrs, and is never a control addr.
- The control link is classified `relayed`/`circuit-relay` on BOTH ends by
  `summarizeConnectionPaths`, and `connection.limits` is absent on every relayed
  connection — the live assertion of the fixture's `applyDefaultLimit: false` parity
  contract (db-p2p's DB protocols do not set `runOnLimitedConnection`, so a limited
  relay would break replication; a regression now fails by name).
- B `addStrand`s the closed row (`FounderOwnerKey: null` → attaches as joiner); its
  strand node reaches A's through the relay from the carried seed alone — **no
  hand-dial anywhere in the file** — both directions gated, both classified relayed
  and unlimited.
- Relay reservation count is **4** (2 control + 2 strand) — the ticket's open cost
  question answered cross-party: one slot per node per network, same number as the
  same-party measurement; party boundaries change nothing.
- The founder-bootstrap `Strand.Header` row becomes visible on B (layer-2 rows crossed
  the circuit), then App rows replicate BOTH ways — including B→A, a total stranger to
  A's party writing into the shared workspace.
- Final sweep on all four nodes: every connection to a non-relay peer is `relayed`, so
  no assertion above could have been served by a direct fallback.

## Use cases to validate against

## Honest gaps / notes for the reviewer

- **The delegate-admission "no-op" assertion is indirect**: protocol absence on the relay
  plus both launches resolving. Nothing instruments `resolveCohortSeed` to prove the
  announce path was entered and folded; a direct probe would need product seams.
- **The >128 KiB / >2 min stream-survival alternative was NOT arranged.** The ticket
  offered it as the expensive form; the `limits === undefined` assertion is the chosen
  cheap form. No bulk-payload write crosses the circuit in this scenario.
- **The control-link relayed assertion runs immediately after `formStrand` resolves** and
  assumes libp2p keeps the formation connection open. Stable across 3 consecutive runs
  (~2-3 s test body); if libp2p ever closes formation connections eagerly this becomes
  flaky, not wrong.
- **`enableRelay: false` on A deviates from the same-party sibling** (which leaves the
  storage-profile default relay server on). Chosen as the truer phone posture; it also
  keeps A's gate on the refuse-outright arm rather than admit-for-relay, which is the arm
  the stranger-exemption assertion actually exercises.
- **Two relays** (each party reserved on a different relay) is out of scope and recorded
  as untested in the scenario header, `docs/strands.md`, `docs/architecture.md`, and the
  `docs/testing.md` uncovered line.
- B never becomes a member of A's party — deliberately no membership/authorization
  assertions; the strand mesh is the only shared surface.
- The full integration suite was not run (agent wall-clock; tracked intermittent families
  in `tickets/.pre-existing-known.md`). Change is purely additive (one scenario + docs).
- `@serfab/cadre-core`'s `dist` was stale at session start (the suite's build-freshness
  guard refused to run) — fixed with a plain `yarn workspace @serfab/cadre-core build`;
  no cadre-core source was touched by this ticket.
