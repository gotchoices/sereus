**How the edit was made.** A script applied exact-substring replacements to the eight lines, and refused to run unless every replaced substring occurred exactly once in its line. So the only text that changed is the list under "Every wording change" below; everything else is the original sentence, in the original order. No sentence was moved.

**The headed-section exception was not used.** Every item has siblings of its kind (other gates, other resolution paths, other wake mechanisms), so none became a heading, and no heading slug changed.

**One item was done although the measurement did not list it.** "Delivery and trust are separate gates" measures exactly 500 words, and the ticket's command tests for more than 500. It was split anyway because the ticket's table names it.

## Known gaps and judgement calls for the reviewer

- **No claim was checked against the code.** The split preserves the text; it does not show the text is still true. Figures such as "23 s at the default", "46 s", "5.5 s", "default 8", and the named functions and scenario files were carried over unread.
- **"What a larger value costs" has no paragraph.** The ticket expected that topic in the dial-budget paragraph. The paragraph does not contain it, so nothing was labelled that way. The topics actually present got the labels: the declared value; the lint rule that enforces it; what is derived from it; what a budget holds besides round trips; what Optimystic derives; how a host overrides it.
- **Two paragraphs are a single sentence of about 200 words** joined by semicolons: "How a reservation is admitted" (connection gate) and "What is derived from it" (dial budgets). Breaking them up means rewriting the sentence, which this ticket's "meaning must not change" argued against. They are one topic each.
- **"What invites carry" opens "…, for the same reason: …"** and the thing it is the same reason as is now in the previous paragraph. Left verbatim because the reason is restated after the colon.
- **Labels are my wording.** Where the original had no marker, the label was chosen from the paragraph's content (for example "What it admits anyway.", "When formation holds it open.", "Scope and failure mode.", "Bounds and forgetting.", "Limits."). "Scope and failure mode." covers four short statements (the stranger-open protocol list, fail-open, outbound dials, strand nodes) that did not each justify a paragraph.
- **The push-wake item's last paragraph opens with two bold runs in a row** ("**Automatic triggering and the platform-push path.** **Who** triggers …"), because the original bolds "Who" for emphasis.
- **In Seed Delivery Protocol the new top-level labelled paragraphs** ("**The pin is supplied at create time.**", "**Proof.**" and so on) sit in a section that also uses bold lines as informal headings ("**Validation**:", "**Alternative Delivery Mechanisms**:"). They are distinguishable by the period and the run-in text, but a reader could take them for siblings.
- **Past-tense or time-marking phrases left in place**, each judged a standing reason or a present fact rather than a story: "checking them would strand records written before the check existed" (why respawn is exempt from the shape check); "from 1.8.0" (a version constraint); "`link-budget.ts` currently derives" (the list is not closed — other deadlines are tagged `link-bound, not yet derived`); "so a recurrence identifies the address that actually failed"; the "(implemented)" tags on every wake mechanism.
- **Not touched, may look stale:** item 4 of the wake list ("Imperative lifecycle") ends "has shipped"; it is under 500 words and outside this ticket. The eleven lines between 350 and 500 words were left alone as the ticket requires.
