# Review: the strand revocation enforcer wakes on a membership change

## What was built

`StrandRevocationEnforcer` (`strand-revocation-enforcer.ts`) no longer polls. Every scheduled refresh runs on one `TableChangeWatch` (`table-change-watch.ts`), the same way `PendingJoinRunner` does:

- **On a change** to `Strand.Member` or `Strand.MemberPeer`, through `StrandDatabase.watchTables`. The instance manager supplies this as the `watchChanges` dep; the table list lives in that closure, next to `readStrandRevocationRows`'s reads.
- **Once at start** (`trigger(0)`).
- **Backstop** `DEFAULT_REVOCATION_BACKSTOP_MS` = 5 min after the last refresh settled.
- **After a failed refresh**, `trigger(REVOCATION_REFRESH_RETRY_MS)` = 30 s.
- **Wide strands:** after a successful refresh whose snapshot has more distinct bound peer ids than `cohortBreadth` (the strand's resolved cluster size), `trigger(REVOCATION_WIDE_STRAND_REFRESH_MS)` = 30 s.
- `refresh()` is still public, serialized on `refreshTail`, and resolves after the sweep. The watch's `onChange` calls it.

The `refreshing` flag, the skip-a-tick logic, `RevocationRefreshScheduler` and `defaultScheduler` are gone. Deps gained `watchChanges?`, `cohortBreadth?` and `scheduler?: TimeoutScheduler`. When `watchChanges` is absent, or `wakeOnChange` is false, or the subscription throws (logged), the enforcer uses `scheduleRuns`.

Config is now `{ enabled?, backstopMs?, wakeOnChange? }`. `pollIntervalMs` and `DEFAULT_REVOCATION_POLL_INTERVAL_MS` were removed with no alias. `index.ts` exports the three new constants and `DEFAULT_MEMBERSHIP_RECONCILE_INTERVAL_MS`, and no longer exports `RevocationRefreshScheduler`.

**Start at publication.** `buildStrandRuntime` no longer calls `start()`. `publishDatabase` calls `this.revocationEnforcers.get(strandId)?.start()` just before the reconciler kick. The enforcer is registered in the map before the database phase, so every publish path reaches it: a launch that was never gated, the gate's `onHeaderHeld`, `ensureFounderBootstrap`, a deferred join's retry, and a hibernation wake (`onWake` → `CadreNode` → `resumeStrand` → `buildStrandRuntime`). `start()` never throws, and is a no-op when already started or stopped. `stop()` disposes the watch, and `StrandDatabase.close()` also disposes it through its registry.

**Reconciler decoupled.** `StrandInstanceManager` passes `config.membershipReconciliation` straight through. The reconciler has its own `DEFAULT_MEMBERSHIP_RECONCILE_INTERVAL_MS` = 30 s, with the same value and meaning as before.

**Docs and comments** were rewritten as the ticket listed: the module doc has a new "When the deny set is refreshed" section, plus the `start` doc and the `readStrandRevocationRows` NOTE; the `revokeMember` / `leaveStrand` docs; both `refreshRevocationEnforcement` docs; `types.ts`; the reconciler docs; strands.md's two removal sections; architecture.md's Consumers list, `revokeMember` bullet and reconciler paragraph. The two required `NOTE:`s sit at `DEFAULT_REVOCATION_BACKSTOP_MS` and at the enforcer's `subscribe()` (the read-repair self-wake).

### Choices beyond the ticket text (please check)

- **A failed `hangUp` also triggers the 30 s retry** (`hangUpRevoked`). Before, the 30 s poll retried a failed hang-up. Without this, a failed hang-up would wait for the 5-minute backstop. One line; the module doc says so.
- **`resolveStrandClusterSize` moved ahead of the enforcer**, out of the `try`, because the enforcer is built before the `try` and needs the breadth. A rejected `clusterSize` throws before anything is allocated, as the `resolveLinkRoundTripMs` check above it already does. `strand-instance-manager-cluster-size.spec.ts` ("rejects a clusterSize below optimystic's minimum without creating a node") still passes. The comment there was rewritten to say so.
- **docs/strands.md → the "non-founder launch" bullet** now says the enforcer starts only at publication.
- **Harness NOTE on `revocationSuspended`:** the enforcer still retries 30 s after a failed read or hang-up, so a "suspended" machine whose start read fails will refresh on its own about 30 s later. See the formation-scenario failure below.

## Use cases to check in review

- A local `revokeMember` on a machine running the strand cuts the removed peers on that machine with no `refreshRevocationEnforcement` call. The write commits on `instance.database.getDatabase()`, the same `Database` the watch is on.
- A replicated removal reaching a cohort member wakes its enforcer: test 2 above, and `strand:revoked` on the removed node.
- Gated joiner: the enforcer starts when the Header arrives, not at build. Before this change the first refresh failed with "no live database" and waited 30 s.
- Hibernation quiesce stops the enforcer and closes the database, disposing the watch; a wake rebuilds both and subscribes again.
- Strand wider than its cluster size: a 30 s follow-up after each successful refresh. At or below the cluster size: none.
- `wakeOnChange: false`: no subscription; refreshes at start, on the backstop, on retry, on the wide-strand follow-up, and on demand.
