# Formation deadlines derived from the declared link

## What changed

Strand formation is the protocol one party's machine runs to join another party's strand: the joiner (initiator) dials the host's control node (responder), sends one contact frame, and waits for one result frame. Every deadline on it was a fixed number chosen on a fast network. They are now all computed by one function, `formationDeadlines(linkRoundTripMs)` in `strand-formation-protocol.ts`, from the machine's declared link round trip (`NetworkConfig.linkRoundTripMs`, default 3 500 ms), using the counts in `link-budget.ts`. With `L` the declaration:

| field | formula | at the default (L = 3 500) | at L = 100 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| dialMs (initiator: open the connection, negotiate the protocol) | 5L + 2 000 | 19 500 | 2 500 |
| awaitContactMs (responder: the contact-frame read) | 2L | 7 000 | 200 |
| validationMs (responder: the token and disclosure reads, counted, not wrapped) | 4L | 14 000 | 400 |
| provisionWorkMs (responder: hook work budget, then its signal aborts) | 10 000 + 24L | 94 000 | 12 400 |
| provisionGraceMs (responder: settle grace, contains one seat read plus one commit) | 22L | 77 000 | 2 200 |
| responseTravelMarginMs (what the initiator's wait holds over the responder's) | 5L | 17 500 | 500 |
| initiatorAwaitResponseMs (initiator: the result-frame read) | 10 000 + 51L | 188 500 | 15 100 |
| sessionMs (both roles) | 12 000 + 56L | 208 000 | 17 600 |

Two new counts in `link-budget.ts`: `PROTOCOL_NEGOTIATION_ROUND_TRIPS = 1` (and `CIRCUIT_REQUEST_ROUND_TRIPS` is now expressed as that plus one) and `COMMIT_ROUND_TRIPS = 20`, a measurement rather than a count. The measurement's figures (optimystic `9e5c1e85`, 2026-09-23, the `delayed` configuration of `relay-round-trip-measure.integration.ts`: one insert took 3.8 to 6.1 s at a 300 ms link round trip, up to 20.3 round trips) are copied into the constant's doc comment, which is now their home, with the instruction to re-measure rather than recompute when `@optimystic/*` is bumped. Two helpers: `relayedStreamOpenBudgetMs` (a relayed dial plus one negotiation, 19 500 ms at the default) and `commitBudgetMs` (70 000 ms at the default).

The dial now runs under `withDeadline`, so an expired dial is cancelled through the signal handed to `dialProtocol`; a stream that arrives after the signal aborted (the dial settled in the same tick the deadline fired) is reset rather than leaked. `stepTimeoutMs` is gone from the initiator's options and replaced by `dialTimeoutMs`; on the listener it still exists and now means the contact-frame read only. Both roles take `linkRoundTripMs`, and `StrandFormationManagerConfig` gains `linkRoundTripMs` and `dialTimeoutMs`. `CadreNode.initializeStrandSolicitation` fills `formationConfig.linkRoundTripMs` from the node's network config unless the caller's formation config already carries one. The approval hook's timeout is exported from `formation-approval.ts` as `DEFAULT_APPROVAL_TIMEOUT_MS`, with its `link-independent` lint reason, and imported by the ladder; no module cycle (the control-database subtree never reaches the protocol). The three retired constants and their four `link-bound, not yet derived` lint directives are gone; `yarn lint` passes with no new directive.

## One deliberate departure from the ticket

The ticket said the responder's clamp reserve "becomes the derived travel margin". That was right when both roles held back the same `stepTimeoutMs`; with per-role rooms it is not enough. The initiator's clamped wait is `session - dial`, and the responder's reply lands `margin` after its provisioning ends, so provisioning must end by `session - dial - margin`; the responder's own room is `session - awaitContact - validation`. The reserve is therefore the difference, `dial + margin - awaitContact - validation` (`responderClampReserveMs`: 16 000 ms at the default, 2 400 at L = 100), floored at zero and still capped at half the room. With the margin alone, a configured budget that clamps only the initiator (or both) at a link under 2 s would put the responder's reply on the wire after the initiator gave up. The responder computes this from its own derivation, since it cannot see the initiator's dial budget; the same-declaration caveat in the `link-budget.ts` module doc covers the mismatch.

A second, smaller one: the clamp now triggers on `requested > ceiling`, not `>=`. The derived defaults sit exactly at the ceiling by construction (session = dial + await-response; the responder's ceiling with the reserve above equals its derived work + grace), so `>=` clamped the defaults to themselves and logged a spurious warning on every listener construction.

## Not run

- The optional relayed confirmation (two relay-only nodes, formation under a 1 500 ms one-way delay) was not run. The claim that the dial now completes there rests on the arithmetic: 19 500 ms against a relayed dial measured at 12 094 ms plus one negotiation of 3 500 ms plus an admission decision of up to 2 000 ms. The formation stream does not set `runOnLimitedConnection`, so a live confirmation needs a relay granting unlimited relayed connections.
- No integration scenario was run. The only integration site that waits on a formation failure (`strand-formation-e2e` Phase 6) configures `provisionTimeoutMs: 3000` on the responder, so its 1 500 / 1 500 work-and-grace split and its assertions are unchanged; only its comment was updated. Every other formation-failure expectation in `packages/integration-tests/src` is a responder-side rejection that arrives well inside the new waits.

## Things a reviewer should look at

- **`openFormationStream`'s late-stream reset** (`strand-formation-protocol.ts`): the `.then` attached before `withTimeout`'s own runs first when the dial and the deadline settle in the same tick, and `signal.aborted` is set before `withTimeout` rejects, so the stream is reset. Verified by reading `withTimeout` and `withDeadline`, not by a test; a test would need a dial double that resolves exactly as a fake timer fires.
- **Cost at the default declaration**: a joiner whose responder accepts the contact and then hangs now waits 188.5 s before being told, where it waited 15 s. The comment on `formationDeadlines` and the Strand Formation section of `docs/architecture.md` both say so. An unreachable responder still fails at the dial (19.5 s).
- **The reserve derivation above**, if the reviewer disagrees that the per-role rooms require it.
- **`validationMs` is counted twice on purpose**: once in the responder's clamp room (it precedes provisioning on the responder's clock) and once in the travel margin (it runs on the initiator's await-response clock before the responder's provisioning clock starts). Those are two different budgets, so it is not double-counting.

## Tripwires recorded

- `DEFAULT_MAX_CONCURRENT_SESSIONS` NOTE (`strand-formation-protocol.ts`) rewritten: a session now holds its slot for up to 171 s at the default declaration; only a caller with a valid token and consent signature reaches provisioning, so the exposure is one invitee opening many sessions with one token. Revisit condition: if invitation tokens are published where untrusted parties can read them, cap in-flight sessions per token.
- `settleWithinGrace` NOTE rewritten: the grace now contains one seat read plus one commit at the declared link; a commit that outlasts even that (a link slower than declared, or a configured budget whose half-cap shrank the grace) still reports "timed out" over a spent invite, and the late-settle log is the observability. Revisit: raise the declared link, not the grace.
- `link-budget.ts` module doc, "A machine that declares a faster link than its peers": formation added. A joiner declaring a faster link than the host derives a shorter await-response than the host's provisioning budget.
