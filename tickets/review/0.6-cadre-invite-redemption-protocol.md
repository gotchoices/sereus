# Redeem a cadre invitation at any member: implementation handoff

## What shipped

The wire protocol, the connection-gate rule and the owner- and device-facing calls the implement ticket specified, additive beside the old `createInvite` / `acceptPhone` / `dialInvite` path and the enrollment window, which `cadre-invitations-redeemable-by-any-member` removes.

- **`cadre-invite-protocol.ts`** (new): the bundle (`CadreInvitation`, `encodeCadreInvitation` / `decodeCadreInvitation` with a full shape check that rejects any other `v`), the two wire messages, the seven codes with `CADRE_INVITE_REJECTION_RETRYABLE`, the three error classes (`CadreInviteRejectedError`, `CadreInviteUnreachableError` carrying per-address outcomes, `CadreInviteReplyInvalidError` for an acceptance the device refuses), `signRedeemRequest` (both signatures over the exact bytes the schema verifies), `verifyRedeemReply`, the member-side `CadreInviteHandler` (checks in cost order: shape, party, connecting identity, both signatures, targeted device; then `seatCadreInvite`, then `redeemCadreInvite`; concurrency cap → `busy`, read timeout; `runOnLimitedConnection` so a phone can redeem through a relay) and the device-side `redeemAtMembers` (one address at a time, each under `relayedRequestBudgetMs`, stop at first accept or first final code).
- **`ControlDatabase`**: `querySeedPeers` (the peer projection moved out of `SeedBootstrapService.queryPeers`, with the pure part exported as `projectSeedPeers`), `listCadreInviteStatuses` (live / withdrawn / usesRecorded per row), and a shared `cadreInviteStillOpen` predicate so `hasLiveCadreInvite` and the listing cannot drift.
- **`SeedBootstrapService`**: `insertCadreInvite` and `withdrawCadreInvite` (owner-key precondition plus signature, as the other owner writes); `queryPeers` delegates to the database; the peer-store merge half of `verifyAndMergeSeed` is now the exported `mergeSeedPeers`, used by the device after redemption.
- **`peer-authorization.ts`**: `verifyCadreInviteRow` and `verifyCadreInviteRedemption` (the base64url verifiers the handler and the device run before any database work).
- **`CadreNode`**: the handler is registered at `start()` after the control database is up and unregistered in `cleanup()`; `admitInboundControlConnection` check 7 (`hasLiveCadreInvite`, inside the same fail-open try as the formation check, reached only on the deny path); `createCadreInvitation` (15 minutes for an untargeted owner grant, 24 hours otherwise, one use, empty anchor refused, members = this machine's resolved invite addresses then `siblingInvitationAddrs`, which is now shared with `createOpenInvitation`), `listCadreInvitations`, `withdrawCadreInvitation`, `redeemCadreInvitation` (party check, pin, sign, dial, verify, merge peers, retain the member as a dial target, refresh the gate, fire a reconcile pass). `OWNER_INVITATION_DEFAULT_TTL_MS` / `CADRE_INVITATION_DEFAULT_TTL_MS` exported.
- **Gate allowlist**: `STRANGER_OPEN_PROTOCOLS` adds `/sereus/cadre-invite/1.0.0`; the module doc has its bullet, including the relay-server posture.
- **Docs**: `docs/architecture.md` gains "Enrollment Flow: Invitation Redeemed at Any Member" (sequence diagram, bundle and request shapes, the codes table, why a keypair, the device's checks and their limit), row 8 of "Which Side Dials", the gate's "What it admits anyway" and "Scope and failure mode" passages, a mint/redeem block under "Helper Functions", and the implementation-status line. The old phone flow and the enrollment window stay in the docs for the next ticket to remove.

## Deviations from the implement ticket, each deliberate

- **The request carries `partyId`.** The ticket's request shape had none, which left `party-mismatch` with no way to be sent: a device dialing a machine of another party would have been answered `issuer-unknown` (retryable) and moved on. The device now states the party it is joining and a machine serving another refuses by name, finally. It discloses nothing new: the bundle the device holds already names the party.
- **The connecting-identity check derives the key from the peer id**, not the peer id from the key. Same 32 bytes compared either way; this reuses `ed25519PublicKeyB64FromPeerId` as the formation listener does rather than adding two libp2p imports.
- **The reply echoes the request's row** rather than re-reading it: after a successful seat or redeem the held row is that row by construction (the key is the primary key and every column is under the issuer's signature), so a read-back would cost one read per redemption to return the same bytes. Stated at the site.
- **A generic `Authorized` refusal at redemption maps to `invite-spent`** (final). That clause covers expired, withdrawn and exhausted, and also "the issuer was removed after the row replicated here" — which the ticket filed under the retryable `issuer-unknown`. When the row is NOT yet held, the seat fails first and is reported `issuer-unknown` as specified; when it is held, an issuer removed since is reported final, which is correct in effect (no member will ever accept it) and saves the device the remaining dials.
- **`reconcileControlCohort()` is fired, not awaited,** at the end of `redeemCadreInvitation`, as the other callers fire it: a pass can spend a whole dial budget on a dead sibling address and the device already holds its connection to the member. A failure is logged.
- **`redeemCadreInvitation` returns `peerId: string | null`**: null only when the answering address named no peer, which a bundle minted by `createCadreInvitation` never does (every address it emits ends in `/p2p/`).
- **The device-side forged-member check is kept but its limit is stated** in the method doc and the architecture doc: the request carries the owner-signed row, so a machine that is not a member can echo it. The check catches a wrong-party or wrong-row answer; the anchor-judged control-database sync is what keeps such a machine from admitting anyone the device trusts.

## Tests added, and what each pins

`cadre-invite-protocol.spec.ts` (real `CadreInviteHandler` over a real control database; the device side dials through a libp2p double that bridges each bundle address into a chosen responder):

- accept end to end: the member holds an invitation-admitted `CadrePeer` row with the device's addresses, the `OwnerKey` row of the grant, one usage row, and lists the device as authorized; the device gets the party id, the same row and dial hints including itself.
- a first address answering `issuer-unknown` (a handler whose seat throws the real `CadreInviteIssuerUnknownError`) is followed by the second, which accepts; dial order asserted.
- a request whose peer key is not the connecting identity is refused `invite-invalid` by a handler whose store throws on any call, and the device never dials the second address.
- an acceptance whose row is signed by a stranger key is refused by the device (`CadreInviteReplyInvalidError`) and the dial stops.
- a reply dropped after the member committed: the retry at the next address is accepted, with exactly one usage row and one peer row.
- an expired invitation is refused `invite-spent` through the engine's own `Authorized` refusal, with no row written.
- every address failing (an unparsable one and an undialable one) throws `CadreInviteUnreachableError` naming both in order.
- encode/decode round-trips a real bundle; another `v`, an empty `ownerKeys`, and a non-base64url string are refused.

`cadre-node-cadre-invitation.spec.ts`:

- defaults by kind (15 minutes and one use for an untargeted owner grant; 24 hours for member-only and for a targeted owner grant; `uses: 0` refused).
- the bundle names this machine's addresses first (alone in its party, exactly those), carries the anchored owner key, and round-trips through its encoding.
- listing shows a minted row live; a withdrawal flips it to withdrawn and not live, returns false the second time, and the row stays.
- an empty anchor is refused before the insert (injected stub); a node without the owner key is refused.
- a started device redeems at a started member over a real TCP connection through the handler `start()` registered: the member lists the device as authorized and holds its owner key; the device pinned the member's owner key, is refused for a wrong party before pinning, and retains the member as a dial target.

`membership-connection-gater.spec.ts`: a stranger is admitted while the fake control database reports a live cadre invitation and denied once it does not (member unaffected); a throwing liveness read fails open; the allowlist test now names all three protocols. The real liveness conditions stay pinned in `control-cadre-invite.spec.ts` (`hasLiveCadreInvite`).

`seed-bootstrap.spec.ts`: the four `queryPeers` cases became three `projectSeedPeers` cases at the pure layer. The fourth ("omits a peer whose stamp is retired") was cut: it asserted a filter the fake database applied to itself; the real exclusion is `queryCadrePeers`' and is pinned by its own spec.

## Known gaps, for the reviewer

- `busy`, `party-mismatch` and `conflict` have no test: the first two are a few lines each (inspection), the third needs a transient multi-node write failure.
- The two-node network test exercises the gate's cold-start admit (the member has no vouched member yet, so its authorized set is empty), not check 7 over the wire. Check 7 is pinned with a fake liveness read plus the real `hasLiveCadreInvite` tests; the wire-level case with a vouched member present belongs to the next ticket's scenario.
- Redemption through a relay (`runOnLimitedConnection` on both ends) is untested over a real relay.
- The device's `CadrePeer` row is written by the member with `Sig` null; the device's own address publish on top of it is the existing joiner behaviour (`registerSelf` self-updates once the row replicates) and is not re-tested here.
- `withdrawCadreInvitation` relies on the existing guarded-delete seam for re-issue after a withdrawal committed alone; not separately tested.
