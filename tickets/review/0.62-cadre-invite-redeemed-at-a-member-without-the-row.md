# Review: redemption at a member that never held the invitation row

## What changed

**Order in `CadreInviteHandler.seatAndRedeem`** is now: check the request (unchanged), decide liveness and push the member's control store to the device (`catchUpDeviceIfLive`), seat the bundle's row (`seatCadreInvite`), redeem. Before, the seat came ahead of the push. The device is in the member's control write cohort as soon as it connects, so the seat is a write it must hold too.

**`ControlDatabase.isCadreInviteLive(invite: CadreInviteRow)`** (was `(key: string)`). It judges the row the node holds under `invite.key`; if none, the passed copy, but only when `verifyCadreInviteRow` accepts its issuer signature. The conditions are unchanged (not withdrawn, unexpired, issuer still an owner here, uses left by the local count). Callers: the handler only (grep confirmed). The seat remains the authority; the pre-check only decides whether the device is sent anything.

The accepted-tradeoff `NOTE:` in `catchUpDeviceIfLive` now also covers a refused seat. Docs: the enrollment section states the order and why (diagram, the "member checks in cost order" sentence, and the paragraph now headed "The device holds the control store before the member writes anything"), names the new scenario as the proof, and links the fork limitation below.

**Harness.** `peerDialGate({ inbound: true })` also refuses inbound connections from a denied peer (`denyInboundEncryptedConnection`), so one side can cut a pair in both directions. Without the option it behaves as before. `startPinningMember`, `admitMember` and `redeemWithRetry` moved from `cadre-invite-any-member` into `harness/cadre-invite.ts`, and both scenarios use them. `phone-owner-never-leaves-members-cohort` edits that scenario's `buildA`; the overlap is small.

## Tests

- `cadre-invite-protocol.spec.ts`, "sends nothing to the holder of a forged row naming a real owner, and the seat refuses it". The row names the founder (an owner) as issuer but is signed by another key, and nothing else would rule it out. It asserts no push, a `issuer-unknown` refusal, and no row seated. This pins the security contract of the reorder: without the signature check in `isCadreInviteLive`, the forger would receive the control store.
- `cadre-invite-row-unreplicated.integration.ts` (new). A owns, M is the member, P is the device. An earlier invitation is minted while A and M are connected, so M holds the `CadreInvite` collection. A is then cut off from M (gate plus hang-up; waits until neither holds a connection and M has left A's cohort), mints an owner-granting invitation and stops. M is checked to hold no row once A has left M's cohort. P redeems and must succeed on the **first** attempt. The test then checks that M holds the bundle's row and lists P as authorized with P's owner key, that P anchored A's key, that P holds the row, and that P lists M as authorized.

## Measured

- New scenario: 4 of 4 passed (P admitted on attempt 1 in 0.7 to 0.8 s). `cadre-invite-any-member` passed alongside it, still with one attempt.
- The scenario fails on the old order: with the seat put back ahead of the push (rebuilt dist), P's first attempt was refused `internal` about 20 s in and P joined on attempt 2 (`expected 2 to be 1`).
- The plan's guess was partly wrong. Without the earlier invitation, M has never held any `CadreInvite` block (the cadre's first invitation creates the collection). The seat then creates the blocks from nothing, does not tear, and the scenario passed on attempt 1 even with the old order. That is why the scenario mints an earlier invitation first. With the old order the tear came back as `internal`, not the `conflict` the plan expected. Both codes are retryable. Not investigated: why `isRetriableControlWriteFailure` does not classify the torn seat.
- A's mint while cut off committed in about 30 ms every run. Optimystic's guard against a freshly isolated node coordinating its own writes never fired, so the scenario does not retry the mint.
- cadre-core full suite: 158 files, 2398 passed, 1 skipped. `yarn lint` and both packages' typechecks are clean.

## Known gaps / for the reviewer

- **Finding: the returning owner cannot mint again.** This is the ticket's optional step 7 (A restarts and sees P). It was run as a temporary extension of the scenario, 3 of 3 runs, and is **not** in the committed file. A lists P as authorized 0.6 to 1.6 s after reconnecting. After that, every `createCadreInvitation` on A fails with `content-digest-mismatch` from the cohort on `default/cadrecontrol/CadreInvite` (24 of 24 attempts over 90 s). `withdrawCadreInvitation` still works. Cause: A's cut-off insert and M's seat are two commits of the same row to one collection, which is the class `blocked/forked-control-collection-sync-livelocks` owns. It is recorded there as the "fourth trigger", with reproduction steps, and noted in the architecture doc. Whether sereus should stop forming this fork (for example, by changing where a member records a row seated from a bundle) instead of waiting for Optimystic is not examined. A reviewer may judge that worth a `blocked/` decision ticket.
- Checked by reading the code only, not by a test:
  - Withdrawn bundle row (the tombstone reached M, the row did not): `isCadreInviteLive` is false, so nothing is pushed. The seat is refused by `NotRevoked`, which maps to `invite-spent`. The plan expected the redemption to give `invite-spent` instead; the code is the same either way.
  - Issuer not an owner here: nothing is pushed, and the seat answers `issuer-unknown`.
  - Row already held: liveness comes from the held row, and the any-member scenario passes in one attempt.
- Concurrent devices redeeming a one-use invitation at a member without the row can both receive the store before one wins. This is the existing accepted tradeoff and has no test.
- The scenario cannot check before A stops that M lacks the row: while the unreachable A is still in M's cohort, M's read of `CadreInvite` failed with `cohort-unreachable`. That was measured in the earlier shape, when M held no `CadreInvite` block. The check runs after M's cohort drops A.
